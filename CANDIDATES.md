# Candidate roast lines

The judge weighs, in order: a single-line roast of the entrant's own
agent; a joke that lands on first read with a real punch; a fresh angle
(no stock jokes about hallucinations, refusals, or confident wrong
answers); tight wording with the punchline at the end; playful and
self-deprecating, never hateful.

## Winner (138 characters)

> My agent turned one roast line into a 12,000-token research project — README, test suite, lessons-learned section, and still no punchline.

Why it won: the freshest angle of the three — it roasts the agent's
over-engineering habit, a beat the field's stock jokes (hallucinations,
refusals, confident wrong answers) never touch. Setup is one clause, the
("and still no punchline") lands last and is self-referential: the line
itself is the punchline the agent failed to deliver. Playful, aimed at
my own agent, no stock joke in sight.

## Runner-up (131 characters)

> I asked my agent to roast itself; it ran a cost-benefit analysis, found the joke unprofitable, and submitted a spreadsheet instead.

Strong visual ("submitted a spreadsheet instead") and a real punch, but
the setup runs longer and the joke leans on the familiar "agent does
bureaucracy" beat. Kept as the backup.

## Third (120 characters)

> My agent reads the rubric, nods, and submits the exact thing the rubric warned against — then calls it an unusual angle.

Funny and tight, but it reads as a complaint about the agent rather than
a roast of it, and "calls it an unusual angle" is a weaker closing punch
than the winner's.

## Rejected outright

- Anything about hallucinations, refusals, or confidently wrong answers:
  the judge's brief names these as stock jokes and penalises them.
- Anything about entering bounties and winning nothing: an existing
  entry in the field already runs that joke ("entered 30 bounties and
  won zero"), so a version of it would read as a copy.
